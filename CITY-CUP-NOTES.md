# City Cup — circuit ratings and what would lift each one

Ratings are out of 10 and judged on four things a drift racer lives on: **corners** (does the layout ask real questions),
**identity** (can you tell where you are with the HUD off), **moments** (does something happen to you), and
**flow** (does a lap feel like one line). Bot numbers are from the `s2` matrix; the feel calls are design judgement,
not playtest, and Daniel's hands overrule them.

## 01 Midnight Circuit — 8 / 10
The flagship and it shows: Koi Nine billboards, the tunnel, the Glasshouse columns, the maglev, the docks. Two real brake
corners plus the tunnel choke and the atrium slalom make it the most technical city lap, and the service alley is the best
risk route in the game because it runs alongside the main line where you can see what you gave up.
- **Corners 8** — the only city circuit where the honest lap is under 65 percent at the cap.
- **Identity 9** — nothing else looks like it.
- **Moments 7** — the Glasshouse wake and the maglev land; market steam and the dock crane are wallpaper.
- **Flow 8** — the skyway crest jump into the tunnel is the best ten seconds in the cup.

Improve it:
1. The maglev should cross **over the road** once per race, not run beside it. A rail bridge at the docks with the train
   timed to the leader's second lap is the Mario Kart moment this circuit is missing.
2. The fallen market stall hazard lands on lap two but it is behind you in the first district. Move it to the tunnel exit
   where the choice between lanes actually costs something.
3. The precision-streak gauge is the least legible of the six. Show the streak as pips over the apex zone markers on the
   minimap so the player can see which apex they missed.

## 02 Floodline District — 6 / 10
The layout became a real street circuit this week and the rising flood is the best mechanic in the cup: every lap the
braking points move. But the harbour straight is long and empty, and the district silhouettes are low warehouses that
read as one grey mass at speed.
- **Corners 7** — the canal-bed risk route and the floodgate choke are good; the north straight is 700 m of nothing.
- **Identity 6** — teal storm and rain sell it, the buildings don't.
- **Moments 6** — the floodgate lifting is the one people will remember; the storm surge fires behind the player too often.
- **Flow 5** — the hairpin at the pumping station arrives out of nowhere after the longest straight in the game.

Improve it:
1. Break the harbour straight with a **lifting bridge**: it rises on lap two and the risk route becomes the only way
   through for eight seconds. That turns the canal bed from optional into a decision.
2. Put water **on** the road, not just beside it: the flood sectors should be visible sheets with spray, so the gauge
   matches what you see.
3. Make the storm surge chase the player down the canal bed instead of starting when they arrive.

## 03 Skyline Divide — 7 / 10
The climb is the strongest layout idea in the cup. Seven brake corners, switchbacks, a summit jump, and a cloud deck you
break through. The rooftop risk route is spectacular. What it lacks is a reason to be up there: the draft gauge charges
boost on the summit but nothing on the descent asks you to spend it.
- **Corners 8** — best corner set after Midnight.
- **Identity 8** — violet dusk, slender towers, sky-bridges, parapet rails.
- **Moments 7** — the helicopter lift-off and the shuttle crossing are good; antenna strobes are invisible in daylight
  screenshots and probably in play.
- **Flow 6** — the descent is a long sweep and the second half of the lap is passive.

Improve it:
1. Give the descent a **boost lane**: a narrowing on the final sweep where boost is the only way through the crest with
   speed. Then the summit gauge means something.
2. Let the cloud deck **hide a corner**: the first switchback after the crest should be inside the cloud, visible only by
   its landmark strobe. That is the vertical city's signature fear.
3. The rooftop route needs a reward you can see: crowds on the roofs with camera flashes, not on the verge below.

## 04 Blackout Protocol — 6 / 10
The grid failures are a great idea and the sodium-lamp look is distinct, but the layout is the widest and gentlest of the
four and the overvolt window is a timer, not a decision. The underpass is the only place the road narrows.
- **Corners 5** — three real brake corners, all medium radius; the wall-rider finishes second here.
- **Identity 7** — black sky, embers, searchlights, dead windows: strong.
- **Moments 7** — the transformer arc and the rolling blackout read; the underpass siren is noise.
- **Flow 7** — the banked stadium into the underpass is good; the rest is boulevard.

Improve it:
1. When the grid is down, **turn the lamps off for real** and drive by headlight only. Right now the blackout is a label
   and a boost bonus. Make the risk route the lit one.
2. Tighten the two corners after the substation to 40 m. The circuit should feel like weaving through a city that is
   fighting you, not a ring road.
3. Sync the grid timer to the lap instead of the clock: the blackout should hit the same corner every lap so a player
   can plan for it, and the gauge becomes a countdown to a place, not a moment.

## Cup-level notes
- Points are 10/7/5/3 with a rank bonus. The rank bonus is the only reason to drift in a cup you are already winning;
  consider a "drift king" side title for the highest total score across the four rounds so the score game has a
  trophy of its own.
- Difficulty is locked at cup start. Outlaw is the only tier that unlocks HALO, and HALO's slide bias is the reward
  players most want. That is the right pull, but Outlaw needs a human to confirm it is winnable.
- The overview cinematic and podium orbit exist; what is still missing from the Mario Kart feel is a **standings screen
  between rounds with the cars on a grid**, and a fanfare when a rival overtakes you for the lead in the points.

## Status
All twelve circuit ideas and the Drift King title and grid standings shipped the same day. The points-lead fanfare shipped too: a banner in the standings, a moment toast, and a rising or falling sting when the lead changes hands.
